# Audience: Dr. Cameron

Dr. Cameron is the first important audience for this project.

She is not just a random viewer. She is the professor and job supervisor who needs to see that the project is moving in a careful, useful direction.

## What the demo should communicate

The demo should make these points clear:

- I started with the backend because the system needs a trusted source of truth.
- The current POC proves the action-to-state-to-review loop.
- Replay and review are the main proof points.
- The scenario is intentionally small and deterministic.
- Validation is POC rule checking, not real emergency guidance.
- Unreal, assets, sensors, and physics are future layers, not current claims.
- Security and trust boundaries are already being considered early.

## Tone

The tone should be:

- clear
- calm
- technically honest
- beginner-friendly
- confident without sounding overhyped

The best framing is:

> Here is the foundation I built, here is what it proves, and here is why the next step is safe.

## What not to do

Do not pitch this as a finished simulator.

Do not imply:

- clinical validity
- official firefighter or EMT protocol authority
- real physics
- real AI decision-making
- production security
- a completed Unreal client

The project is strongest when it is honest about being early.

## Best 10-minute flow

Use 5 minutes for explanation and 5 minutes for demo.

1. Explain the POC loop.
2. Explain why the backend owns truth.
3. Show the local demo.
4. Point out replay verification.
5. Point out review summaries.
6. Point out warnings/errors when cues are ignored.
7. End with the next step.
